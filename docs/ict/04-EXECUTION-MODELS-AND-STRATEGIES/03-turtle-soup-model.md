# Level 04: Execution Models & Strategies
## Module 03: The Turtle Soup Strategy (Liquidity Purge & Revert)

---

### 1. The Strategy That Punishes Breakout Traders

In the 1980s, the famous "Turtle Traders" trend-following experiment taught traders to buy 20-day high breakouts and sell 20-day low breakouts.

Recognizing that institutional algorithms frequently exploit this retail habit, legendary market technician Larry Williams formulated the **Turtle Soup** setup. Michael J. Huddleston (ICT) heavily refined and institutionalized this model, defining it as the definitive **"Liquidity Purge and Revert"** setup.

```
                   TURTLE SOUP SHORT (BEARISH PURGE)
                   
                     [ FALSE BREAKOUT WICK ]
                        ▲  Sweeps 20-day High / EQH!
                       ┌┴┐ Retail breakout orders triggered.
                       │ │ Short stop losses purged.
  PREVIOUS 20-PERIOD   │ │
  HIGH / EQUAL HIGHS  ─┼─┼─────────────────────────────────────────────
                       │ │ Candle body REJECTS and closes BACK INSIDE!
                       └┬┘
                        ▼  ENTRY TRIGGER: Price re-enters the range.
                       ┌┴┐
                       │ │
                       │ │ AGGRESSIVE COLLAPSE TOWARD DISCOUNT LIQUIDITY!
                       └─┘
```

---

### 2. The Core Anatomy of Turtle Soup

A **Turtle Soup** setup is an intentional stop run that lacks follow-through.

#### A. Turtle Soup Short (Bearish Setup):
1. **The Benchmark Level:** Identify a prominent high (e.g., a 20-period high, Previous Day High, or clean Equal Highs).
2. **The Purge:** Price rallies strongly and breaks *above* the benchmark level.
3. **The Trap:** The expansion immediately runs out of gas. Instead of holding above the broken resistance, price prints a long wick and closes **back inside the prior range**.
4. **The Entry:** Sell short as price crosses back below the benchmark high (or upon the close of the rejection candle).
5. **Stop Loss:** Placed strictly above the tip of the fakeout wick.
6. **Take Profit:** Target the internal discount array or the opposing low.

#### B. Turtle Soup Long (Bullish Setup):
1. **The Benchmark Level:** Identify a prominent low (e.g., 20-period low, Previous Day Low, or Equal Lows).
2. **The Purge:** Price drops below the benchmark low, triggering sell stops.
3. **The Trap:** The candle violently rejects with a lower wick and closes **back above the benchmark level**.
4. **The Entry:** Buy long as price crosses back above the level.
5. **Stop Loss:** Placed strictly below the tip of the fakeout wick.
6. **Take Profit:** Target the opposing high.

---

### 3. Turtle Soup vs. "Turtle Soup Plus One" (TS+1)

- **Standard Turtle Soup:** The false breakout and the rejection/re-entry occur on the **very same day / candle**.
- **Turtle Soup Plus One (TS+1):** 
  - Day 1: Price breaks beyond the 20-day high/low and closes outside the range (tempting retail traders to hold overnight).
  - Day 2 (The "+1"): Price immediately collapses back inside the original range, trapping all Day 1 breakout buyers. 
  - TS+1 often produces an even more catastrophic squeeze because overnight breakout positions panic-sell simultaneously.

---

### 4. Golden Rules for High-Probability Turtle Soup

1. **Check the HTF Context:** Turtle Soup is most lethal when the sweep occurs directly into a **Higher-Timeframe PD Array** (e.g., sweeping yesterday's high directly into a Weekly Bearish Order Block).
2. **Beware of Macro News Runs:** Never attempt a Turtle Soup against a high-impact news catalyst (e.g. FOMC rate announcement or CPI). When the algorithm is executing a true macro repricing, breakout candles will NOT re-enter the range—they will run away.
3. **Wick vs. Body Discipline:**
   - A wick poking beyond the level = **High-Probability Turtle Soup**.
   - Multiple consecutive candle bodies closing outside the level = **Breakout trend. DO NOT FADE.**
